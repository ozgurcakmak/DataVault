I remember the days when "free" meant shitty. If you want to use something, read something, listen to something - you paid. Anything that was free, had some sort of deficiency or some lack.

Then Google launched Gmail and offered it for free. This was an era where Hotmail and Yahoo gave you 5-10 MB; Google offered "unlimited storage". And it was good - actually good! The interface was simple, fast and was a fresh change from the others. And the storage claim? They actually meant it - there even was a counter showing the storage area you currently have. And it was always ticking upward, proving it.

We used MySpace for free, Facebook for free, Friendfeed, Tumblr and so on were free. The assumption was "oh they sell ads, don't they" but the fact was more insidious. The content we provided was the honey. Nobody likes to come to a ghost town, you only draw new users by providing a vibrant and active community. We were the ad.

Now most platforms offer "premium" plans too. To provide an "ad-free" experience - as if ads were the problem. What a joke! This feels like a triple extortion. First, my presence. Then my content. Now my money.

Nowadays, I am leery of any resource that proudly proclaims itself as "free". As Heinlein put it - and he wasn't wrong - "There ain't no such thing as a free lunch". The moment you use a service for free, you are left to their whims. Try to delete your content. You can't, because the platform needs it to 'draw people in'. It is not just 'your' content anymore. You cannot have a say, because you're not a stakeholder - you're _just_ a freeholder.

Free used to mean shitty. Now it means you're the product - and the factory, and the raw material. Thanks, but either I pay and have a say, or I'll take my shitty up front.
