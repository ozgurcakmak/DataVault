I recently read an article with the title "Humanity isn't ready for the coming intelligence explosion" and it made me think. Not the article itself - futurism always has a 50% chance of being wrong. But is it "the intelligence explosion" we fear or is it something else?

The use of technology and automation in workplace is not a new thing. Since the 1980s we are working side by side with robots. They are lauded for increasing productivity, decreasing work related accidents and they are more efficient. But the critical part is they didn't look like human and they certainly did not respond like human beings. The AI does. Herein lies the threat because our minds are wired to assess the environment for threats, it can be flora or fauna but mostly it is other human beings. When something talks like a human, responds like a human and seemingly faster and more efficient than a human... we get angsty.

Also the decades of cultural wiring do not help. From Colossus: The Forbin Project to Terminator (with the only exception of Iain M. Banks' The Culture series) our sci-fi treated AI and robots as things that are against us. And here comes a product (because the AI was not a new technology, not by any stretch) that feels akin to what we have read so far and we respond appropriately.

Like the seamstrresses of the old times, whose craft was undone by the sewing machine, what we do is mostly rote work. The e-mails we send, the code we write and the things we create at work are usually some derivative of a previously accepted design. That's why there are "approved designs", corporate logos, letterheads. Even if you were the next Salvador Dali, the moment you work for Company(TM) you have to use their designs, their colors, their logo. AI makes this easier.

I think if you are using AI for your persoal creative endeavors, you are not using it right. And/or if you see the output AI Generated for you directly, without any checking and editing, you are not using it right. As they have internal guardrails, so should we have guardrails on how we use them.
